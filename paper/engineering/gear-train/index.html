A two-stage gear train for machine design.

A double-reduction spur gear train that takes 21 hp at 735 rpm down to 216 rpm. I chose the tooth counts and checked the design through to critical speed.

Baylor, EGR 3323 · Spring 2026

The gear train

Both stages on their shafts. The bearings are plain rings at their real sizes, and the short input shaft is a stand-in size.

Loading the model

The brief was 21 hp in at 735 rpm, 216 rpm out, on 11 inch shafts, with a design life of 260 million revolutions.

I picked tooth counts from the preferred list, 60 to 140 and then 96 to 140, which lands on 216.0 rpm exactly.

  1. 735 rpmin
  2. 60 to 140first stage
  3. 315 rpmbetween stages
  4. 96 to 140second stage
  5. 216.0 rpmout

From there I worked through:

  1. 01Torques and gear forces
  2. 02Bearing loads
  3. 03Bearing selection
  4. 04Gear tooth strength
  5. 05Shaft fatigue and yield
  6. 06Deflection
  7. 07Slope
  8. 08Critical speed

The face width took two tries.

1 in
My first face width left Gear D with a factor of safety of 1.02, under the 1.5 required.
1.5 in
At 1.5 inches, every gear cleared 2.1.

The bearings ended up setting the shaft sizes. The shafts came out with fatigue factors of safety of 4.5 and 8.7. The tightest spot was the slope at Gear E, 0.024 degrees against a 0.03 degree limit.

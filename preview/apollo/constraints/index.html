What it taught me

What I learned between the first commit and 98 members

Eight lessons from decisions I made along the way.

What it taught me · Waco, Texas · June 5 to October 24, 2026

Timeline

  1. June 5, 2026First commit.
  2. June 8, 2026First member account.
  3. September 8, 2026Live on Google Play.
  4. September 13, 2026Approved on the App Store.
  5. September 14, 2026Launch night with the Baylor Driving Club.
  6. September 17, 2026Clubs ship.
  7. October 1, 2026First shop tool, an inventory board.
  8. October 5, 2026Shop tools rebuilt around bookings.
  9. October 24, 202698 members.

Engineering lessons

Constraints decide the design

I was one builder with two app stores to ship to. Three native apps weren't realistic, so I built one web app inside native shells. The push bridge, the test suite and the release path all followed from that one constraint.

Find the limit before you build

My first shop tool tracked inventory, because that's what I assumed shops needed. Watching a shop work showed me the limit was bay time. Four days later the tools were rebuilt around bookings.

Measure before you assume

The live AI service didn't always behave the way its documentation said. I stopped trusting the docs, checked behavior against the live service and wrote the tests around what it did.

Put rules where they can't be skipped

A rule in one screen protects one screen. Putting the content filter in the database means every message, club and profile goes through it, including screens that don't exist yet.

Never let a model grade itself

AI output is checked by code, schemas and tests. When an answer matters, something outside the model decides if it's right.

Show only what's confirmed

Job times stay hidden until the shop confirms them, and a part Smart Add isn't sure about stays separate and doesn't get merged. A wrong answer shown with confidence costs more trust than an honest blank.

Prove the whole path

Two halves that each work on their own don't mean the whole thing works. I check the full path, from a member's tap to what lands on the other side.

Users change the plan

On launch night people used the assistant first, so I built the product around that and changed my original plan.

Shop software

Booking software that plans a shop around bay time

A booking calendar, server-priced estimates, inventory and staff accounts for wrap, tint and film shops.

The pivot

I started on October 1, 2026 with an inventory board: stock counts, reorder points and supplier links. Watching a shop work showed me that the limit is time in the bay. A full wrap holds a bay for days, and no amount of stock changes that.

On October 5, 2026 I rebuilt the tools around bookings. Every job has a length, from a quick job to three days or more, and it holds its bay every working day it runs. A three-day wrap that starts on a Thursday holds Friday and Monday and skips the weekend. Availability is read from the shop's own board, so what a customer can book never drifts from what the shop has promised.

The booking engine

A calendar that follows the car

A car shows on every day from drop-off to pickup, and cars without dates sit in their own line. Start times fall on the half hour and stop an hour before close, so no job is booked into a closed shop. Job times stay hidden until the shop confirms them.

Prices the server owns

The customer's browser sends only the line items. The server prices them from the shop's own list in whole cents, with rates in basis points, and ignores any total it was sent, so a price can't be changed on the way in.

The 3D Visualizer

Beta

Customers try a tint, wrap or film on a 3D car and send it as a quote request. The server prices it from the shop's list, and anything it can't match goes to the shop as a question.

Running a shop on it

Owners and staff

Owners and staff sign in through separate links. Passwords are hashed with scrypt, only a hash of each session is stored, and the system won't let a shop end up with no active owner.

Inventory with a paper trail

Counts change with a tap, reorder points flag low stock, and every change is logged. The shop is taken from the signed-in session and ignores what the request says, so one shop can't touch another's stock.

A demo in their own name

A shop I'm talking to can try the full product under its own name and logo before signing up. The server decides what's a demo, and a demo never writes, so a sales call can't create a real booking.

Billing

Built, in testing

Shop subscriptions run through Stripe. Every billing event's signature is checked on the server before it can change a plan.

Where it stands

One shop is running it in a pilot, as of October 24, 2026.
